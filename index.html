Silo Math

The bunker of 80 ft by 30 ft wide by 10 ft tall holds 24000 cu ft of silage.

Boundary: arithmetic only Silo Math counts silage with multiplication and division: the volume a bunker holds, the pounds and tons at a density, the days a herd eats through the pile, and the face removal in inches a day. It applies the counting as written, so it has not seen your bunker and does not know your packing, your moisture, your shrink, your spoilage or your ration, and it gives no verdict that a density is safe or a rate is enough. Every number here is your own entry.

Starting examples: an 80 x 30 ft bunker piled 10 ft, at 40 pounds a cubic foot, feeding 100 head at 50 pounds each a day.

The volume and tonnage truths

The bunker of 80 ft by 30 ft wide by 10 ft tall holds 24000 cu ft, and at 40 pounds a cubic foot that makes 960000 pounds, or 480 tons of silage.

The days truth

The 960000 pounds feeding 100 head at 50 pounds each a day last 192 days.

The face truth

The herd of 100 head at 50 pounds each eats 125 cu ft a day off the 300 sq ft face, or 5 inches a day.

Run your numbers
Silo Math is exact arithmetic over the numbers you enter, nothing more.
